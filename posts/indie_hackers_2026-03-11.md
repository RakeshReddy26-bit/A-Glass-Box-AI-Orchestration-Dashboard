This week I dove deep into how AI career platforms are evolving, and honestly, it's making me rethink SkillVector's approach.

Competitors are building integrated coding environments for live skill verification - essentially turning job interviews into real-time model training sessions. Meanwhile, others are automatically tracking deployed model performance to generate verified portfolios. It's impressive tech, but I think they're solving the wrong problem.

The real insight came from salary data: while traditional ML roles have plateaued, specialized AI safety positions are seeing 40% premiums. Companies are desperate for people who understand both the technical and ethical implications of AI systems.

This taught me something important about building SkillVector. Instead of competing on flashy real-time coding assessments, we should focus on mapping these emerging skill transitions. Help engineers understand not just what they know, but what they should learn next based on where the market is heading.

The challenge is that this requires much more sophisticated intelligence gathering than I initially planned. We're essentially building a real-time labor market observatory, not just a career tool. More complex, but potentially much more valuable.

Sometimes the best product insights come from watching competitors and realizing you want to build something completely different.
