This week I dove deep into AI recruiting platforms for SkillVector research and honestly, it's both exciting and terrifying.

Companies like DeepHire are now doing live coding evaluations instead of traditional interviews. Major tech firms are requiring verified skill badges for senior ML roles. The days of resume keyword optimization are ending.

What I learned building SkillVector: The hardest part isn't the AI or the graph database complexity - it's getting the career intelligence right. We're using Claude Sonnet to analyze job market trends, Neo4j for skill relationships, and Pinecone for semantic matching, but the real challenge is making actionable insights from noisy market data.

I spent 3 days debugging why our skill gap analysis was showing inflated salaries, only to realize we were double-counting remote-first companies in multiple locations. These data quality issues are brutal but teach you so much about the actual job market.

The breakthrough moment: Realizing that skill verification platforms validate our core thesis. Career success isn't about what you claim to know - it's about proving real capabilities and staying ahead of market shifts.

Still lots of work ahead, but seeing the market move toward verified skills gives me confidence we're building something that matters.
