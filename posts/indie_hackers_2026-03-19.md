This week I dove deep into job market data for SkillVector and discovered something counterintuitive about the AI boom.

While everyone talks about AI creating jobs, the data shows a massive bifurcation. Senior ML engineers with production experience are getting $280K-350K offers, but entry-level positions dropped 40%. Companies have completely shifted from hiring researchers to seeking engineers who can deploy and scale models.

This insight is reshaping how we're building SkillVector. Instead of generic "learn AI" recommendations, we're focusing on the specific gap between academic ML knowledge and production deployment skills. Our users need to understand that MLOps, model optimization, and distributed systems experience are the real differentiators.

The technical challenge has been integrating real-time job market analysis with our skill gap detection. We're using Pinecone to match job requirements with user profiles and Neo4j to map skill relationships, but the complexity of parsing and weighting production experience versus academic credentials is harder than expected.

The brutal truth: most AI career advice is outdated. The market has moved beyond "learn Python and TensorFlow" to demanding proven ability to scale models in production environments.

Building in public means sharing these uncomfortable realities. The AI job market isn't what people think it is.
