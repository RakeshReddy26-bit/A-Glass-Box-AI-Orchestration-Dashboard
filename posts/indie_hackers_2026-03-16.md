This week I realized we're solving the wrong problem at SkillVector.

I've been obsessing over building the perfect skill gap analysis algorithm, trying to match job descriptions with user profiles. Classic founder mistake - falling in love with the solution instead of the problem.

Then GitHub launched their AI Career Copilot, and it hit me: the future isn't about better job matching. It's about understanding the dynamic relationship between what you're building today and where the market is heading tomorrow.

So we're pivoting our core engine. Instead of static skill assessments, we're building real-time career intelligence that tracks emerging technology trends, salary movements, and hiring patterns to predict which skills will matter in 6-12 months.

The technical challenge is gnarly - we're combining Neo4j for relationship mapping, Pinecone for semantic search across job postings, and Claude for contextual analysis. But early users are responding way better to "here's what to learn next based on market trends" vs "here are jobs that match your current skills."

Hardest part? Convincing people their career strategy should be as data-driven as their product decisions. We're so used to wing-it career planning that intentional, measurable growth feels foreign.

Revenue is still tiny, but retention doubled since the pivot. Sometimes you need to build the wrong thing to understand the right problem.
