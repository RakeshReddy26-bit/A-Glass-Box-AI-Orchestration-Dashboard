This week hit me with a reality check about what we're actually solving at SkillVector.

GitHub launched Copilot Careers - analyzing real commit history to match developers with ML roles. Anthropic released Claude Career Coach for personalized ML guidance. Meanwhile, ML hiring surged 340% but it's all production-focused roles while bootcamp graduates flood junior positions.

Here's what I learned building our skill intelligence platform: everyone's trying to solve career matching, but they're missing the nuance. It's not just about matching skills to jobs - it's about understanding the massive gap between what people learn and what companies actually need.

Our biggest challenge this week was modeling the relationship between theoretical ML knowledge and production skills in our knowledge graph. How do you quantify the difference between someone who can train a model in a notebook vs someone who can deploy it at scale?

We're using Neo4j to map these skill relationships, but honestly, the hardest part isn't the tech - it's defining what "production-ready ML skills" actually means across different companies and contexts.

The data is clear: senior MLOps engineers are getting 25-40% raises while junior roles face brutal competition. The market is screaming for production deployment skills, but most learning resources still focus on the research side.

That's our edge - bridging this gap with real career intelligence.
