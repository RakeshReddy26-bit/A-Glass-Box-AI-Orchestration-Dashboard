This week I learned something humbling while building SkillVector: our users aren't just looking for skill assessments - they want to understand which skills actually matter.

We initially built our ML assessment engine focusing on traditional algorithms and model architectures. But after analyzing 10,000+ job postings and talking to hiring managers, I realized we were solving the wrong problem.

The real insight: 40% of top tech companies now use AI-assisted coding sessions instead of whiteboard interviews. They care more about how you collaborate with AI tools than whether you can implement gradient descent from scratch.

So we pivoted our assessment framework. Instead of testing pure algorithmic knowledge, we now evaluate:
• AI-augmented problem solving
• System design with modern ML infrastructure
• Real-world debugging scenarios

The challenge? Building assessments that feel authentic while being scalable. We're using Claude Sonnet to generate dynamic scenarios, but maintaining consistency across evaluations is tough.

Revenue is still early ($3K MRR), but user engagement jumped 40% after this shift. Sometimes the market teaches you what you should have built in the first place.
