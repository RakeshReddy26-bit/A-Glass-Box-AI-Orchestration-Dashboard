This week I had a humbling realization while building SkillVector.

I was obsessing over our graph database architecture and vector similarity algorithms—classic founder tunnel vision. Then I noticed something in our user behavior data: people weren't struggling with the technical features. They were struggling to understand what the insights actually meant for their careers.

A user messaged: "Your platform says I need MLOps skills, but I don't even know what that looks like day-to-day." It hit me—we built a sophisticated AI career intelligence system but forgot the human context layer.

So we pivoted this week. Instead of just flagging skill gaps, we're now providing concrete examples of what these skills look like in practice. Real job scenarios, actual problems these skills solve, salary ranges for specific combinations.

The GitHub AI Career Copilot launch reinforced this. People don't just want to know their gaps—they want to see exactly how to bridge them and what success looks like.

Building with Claude Sonnet has been incredible for processing these nuanced career insights, but the real breakthrough came from remembering that behind every data point is someone trying to figure out their next career move.

Sometimes the most important feature is the explanation, not the algorithm.
